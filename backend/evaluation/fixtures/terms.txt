Atlas Studio — controlled service terms, revision 1
Who may register. Individual users must be at least 18 years old to create an Atlas Studio account. A person creating an account for an organisation must also have authority to accept the service terms for that organisation. The age rule applies to individual accounts regardless of whether a free trial or paid plan is selected. Keeping login credentials private is the account holder's responsibility. Shared team work should use separate member accounts instead of a common password. An account invitation from an administrator does not waive the minimum age requirement. Contact details must remain accurate so security and billing notices can reach the responsible account holder.

Trial period. A new workspace may use a 14-day trial without supplying a payment card. The trial starts when the workspace is created and ends at the same time of day 14 calendar days later. At expiry, editing is disabled until the owner chooses a paid plan. A trial does not convert automatically into a paid subscription, and the service does not charge a card merely because the owner viewed the pricing page. Trial work can still be exported during the following seven days. Support may explain plan differences but cannot extend a trial by treating a renamed workspace as a new customer. These trial rules are separate from renewal arrangements for an already paid workspace.

Paid-plan renewal. Monthly paid plans renew on the same calendar day each month, with the final day of the month used if that day does not exist. The workspace owner can disable renewal in billing settings before the renewal time. Cancellation takes effect at the end of the paid period; members retain their plan features until then. Monthly fees already paid are not refunded solely because the owner stopped using the service mid-month. Cancelling renewal does not delete workspace content. The owner receives an email confirmation and should keep it if a billing dispute later arises. An administrator without billing permissions must ask the owner to change the renewal setting.

Annual plan changes. An owner on an annual plan may request a downgrade at any time, but the lower plan begins only when the current annual term ends. A downgrade does not create a prorated refund for unused months. Extra seats added during an annual term are charged for the remaining part of that term and join the same renewal date. Removing a seat prevents its renewal but does not erase content authored by that member. Support records the requested plan so the owner can check the change before the next invoice. Annual billing rules do not turn an unpaid trial into an annual commitment; the owner must explicitly choose and purchase the annual plan.

Customer content licence. Customers retain ownership of the documents and designs they upload. They grant Atlas a limited licence to store, process and display that content solely to provide the service and carry out the customer's sharing instructions. The licence does not permit Atlas to sell a customer's private designs as stock assets. Publishing a share link allows access to the selected material under the link's settings; it does not transfer ownership to a viewer. Customers must hold the rights needed for uploaded content. If a customer removes a public share link, Atlas stops offering access through that link, although a recipient may already have downloaded a permitted copy.

Suspension process. Atlas may suspend access immediately when necessary to contain an active security incident, such as a compromised account distributing malicious links. For an ordinary first-time billing failure, Atlas instead sends a notice and allows seven calendar days to update payment details before suspension. A suspended workspace cannot edit or create share links, but its owner can contact support to resolve the stated cause. These two situations use different response times because a live security incident can harm other users while a failed payment normally does not. An owner should not interpret a payment grace period as permission to continue activity that threatens the service.

Ending an account. An owner who chooses permanent workspace closure has 30 calendar days after closure to request a content export. After that window, the active workspace content is scheduled for deletion and Atlas no longer promises an export. Closure affects the entire workspace, whereas cancelling paid-plan renewal only changes future billing. The closing owner is responsible for notifying other members and keeping any required copies. Content under an active dispute hold may be retained separately until that dispute is resolved. Requesting an export does not reactivate a closed workspace or start a new paid term. Support verifies the owner's identity before releasing a closure archive.

Service availability. Maintenance notices are posted on the status page when planned work is expected to interrupt access. Atlas aims to reduce disruption, but these terms do not promise uninterrupted operation or a fixed uptime percentage. Customers should keep copies of critical work and avoid relying on an unsaved browser tab as their only record. A status incident explains the scope of an outage and may be updated as the investigation proceeds. Reporting an outage does not itself create a refund, service credit or extension of a paid term. The support team prioritises restoring access and then publishes relevant incident information when it is available.
