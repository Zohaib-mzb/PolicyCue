Meadow Forum — controlled community guidelines, revision 1
Criticism and harassment. Members may criticise ideas, products and public decisions, including strongly worded disagreement, as long as they do not direct threats or sustained personal abuse at another person. Repeatedly tagging someone after they have asked for contact to stop is treated as targeted harassment. Disagreement with a moderator's decision is not itself harassment and may be raised through the appeal process. Members should quote only the material needed to explain a criticism rather than repost a private conversation in full. Moderators consider the surrounding discussion when deciding whether language attacks an idea or targets a person. Popularity of a post does not excuse prohibited conduct.

Private information. Posting another person's home address, private phone number or identity-document number without their consent is prohibited, even if the information was obtained from a different website. Removing one digit does not make an otherwise identifiable private phone number acceptable. Members can discuss public business contact information when relevant to the discussion, but should not use it to organise harassment. If private information is posted accidentally, the author should remove it promptly and report the incident so moderators can review copies. A member's willingness to share one contact detail does not imply consent to publish their other private information.

Promotional posts. Members may share their own project in the weekly showcase thread, with a clear statement of their connection to the project. The same promotional message may not be copied into unrelated discussion threads. Affiliate links must be marked as affiliate links before a reader follows them. Ordinary recommendations made without payment or affiliation do not require a promotional label, though they still must fit the discussion. Moderators may move an otherwise acceptable project announcement into the showcase thread. Disclosure makes a permitted promotion transparent; it does not authorise spam, impersonation or misleading claims about a product's capabilities.

Graphic content. Realistic graphic injury images are not allowed in public posts or profile pictures. A text discussion of an accident or safety incident is allowed if it avoids gratuitous descriptions and does not identify a private victim without consent. Educational intent does not override the ban on graphic injury images in public areas. Members reporting such an image should use the report control rather than quote or reupload it into a complaint thread. Moderators may preserve a restricted internal copy to review the report. A simple safety diagram without realistic injury detail is not treated as a graphic injury image under this rule.

Reporting and review. The report control sends a post and its surrounding discussion to the moderation queue. Reports should identify the suspected rule violation and relevant context, rather than recruit other members to submit duplicate reports. Moderators assess the content, not merely the number of reports received. A report acknowledgement confirms receipt and does not mean the reported member has been found at fault. Members facing an immediate real-world emergency should contact appropriate local emergency services instead of waiting for forum moderation. The forum does not guarantee a fixed response time for ordinary reports; urgency and available evidence influence the review order.

Enforcement ladder. For an ordinary first violation, moderators normally remove the offending content and send a warning explaining the rule. A second ordinary violation within 90 days results in a seven-day posting restriction. A credible threat of violence can instead result in an immediate permanent ban, without a prior warning. Restrictions are tied to the account's conduct, not the popularity of the member. Trying to evade a restriction with another account is a separate violation. Moderators record which rule was applied so the affected member can understand the decision. The ordinary ladder does not limit action needed to protect a person from a credible threat.

Appeals. An affected member may appeal a moderation decision within 14 calendar days of the decision notice. The appeal should identify the decision and explain the relevant context or claimed error. A moderator who did not make the original decision reviews the appeal. The existing restriction remains in force while the appeal is considered unless the review team explicitly lifts it. Repeatedly reposting removed content is not an appeal and may create another violation. A successful appeal may restore content or shorten a restriction, depending on the finding. Members receive the outcome through the account inbox rather than through a public vote in the discussion thread.

Automated participation. Bots may post only in channels whose moderators have explicitly approved that bot, and every automated post must be visibly labelled as automated. Approval for one channel does not authorise posting across the entire forum. A human operator remains responsible for the bot's conduct and must provide a working contact route to moderators. Bots must not impersonate ordinary members or send unsolicited private promotional messages. Moderators can revoke channel approval when a bot becomes disruptive even if its posts are labelled. Technical availability of an account token does not count as permission to operate a bot, and a labelled post must still follow all content rules.
