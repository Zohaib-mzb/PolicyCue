Lumen Assist — controlled AI usage policy, revision 1
Feature boundaries. Lumen provides optional drafting and summarisation tools inside a workspace. These tools suggest text for a user to review; they do not independently publish a document, submit an application or send a message to an outside recipient. Turning off the AI feature leaves ordinary manual editing available. An administrator can enable the feature for a workspace, but each member still decides whether to submit a particular prompt. A generated suggestion is not an instruction from the workspace administrator. Members should consider the task and the information being submitted before asking for a suggestion, rather than assume every document is suitable for AI processing.

Prompt handling. When a member uses AI, the selected text and the prompt are sent to a contracted model provider to produce the requested response. Lumen does not use customer prompts or outputs to train its own general-purpose models, and its provider contract prohibits training the provider's general-purpose models on that submitted content. This restriction concerns model training, not the processing needed to generate a response. The member should submit only the text needed for the task. Ordinary workspace documents are not sent to the model provider simply because AI is enabled; a member must invoke the feature with selected material or a prompt.

Operational retention. Prompt and output logs are retained for 30 days for abuse investigation and service troubleshooting, then deleted unless they are part of an active abuse investigation. An investigation hold lasts until that investigation is closed and is restricted to staff handling it. This logging schedule is distinct from the customer's decision to save a generated draft as a normal workspace document. Deleting a saved draft does not immediately erase an operational log still inside its retention period. Access to held logs is recorded, and the material may not be reused as a public demonstration. A training prohibition should not be interpreted as a promise that no operational records ever exist.

Sensitive inputs. Members must not submit passwords, authentication tokens, complete payment-card numbers or another person's medical records to the drafting tool. Replacing a person's name with initials does not make an otherwise identifiable medical record acceptable. Publicly available product specifications may be submitted when the member has permission to use them. An administrator enabling AI cannot waive these input restrictions. If a secret is submitted accidentally, the member should rotate the secret where appropriate and notify the security contact so access to the submitted material can be reviewed. Removing a saved response alone may not contain exposure of a credential that was already sent.

Review of outputs. A member must check generated facts and any quoted sources before sharing an AI draft outside the workspace. AI text can contain errors, omit qualifications or describe a source inaccurately. The tool does not certify that a citation exists merely because it appears in a fluent answer. Editing a draft remains the member's responsibility, including checking that it does not reveal confidential information from selected source material. The interface offers a copy action for convenience, not a declaration that the output has passed review. A colleague's approval may be useful, but the policy does not replace the member's duty to check the relevant facts and sources.

High-impact decisions. AI output must not be the sole basis for deciding whether a person receives employment, credit, housing or access to an essential service. A qualified human must assess the underlying evidence and be responsible for such a decision. Lumen may help organise non-sensitive background material for review, but the generated summary cannot substitute for that assessment. An administrator's instruction to use the tool does not remove the requirement for human responsibility. This restriction applies even if the output appears confident or gives a numerical ranking. It is intended to prevent an unreviewed generated suggestion from becoming the final decision about a person's opportunity.

Generated media disclosure. When a member shares an AI-generated image publicly as part of a campaign, the image or its accompanying caption must clearly disclose that it was AI-generated. A private internal draft that is not distributed publicly does not require this public disclosure label. Disclosure does not permit impersonating a real person or falsely presenting a fabricated event as a documented occurrence. A member who substantially edits a generated image must still disclose its AI origin when using it in the public campaign. The disclosure rule concerns the audience and use of the image, while factual review and permissions remain separate responsibilities.

Feature incidents. Members can report unsafe or unexpectedly revealing AI output through the feedback control, including the minimum context needed to investigate. Reports should not reproduce a secret in an open team channel. An administrator may temporarily disable AI for the workspace while a report is investigated; manual editing and access to existing documents continue. Re-enabling the feature does not approve a previously unsafe prompt or remove restrictions on sensitive information. Support reviews incident reports to improve service handling, but a submitted report does not guarantee an immediate change to the underlying model. Users should stop relying on a suspect output while its accuracy or disclosure risk is unresolved.
