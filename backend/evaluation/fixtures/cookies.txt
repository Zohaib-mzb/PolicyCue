Cedar Library — controlled cookie and tracking policy, revision 1
Essential session cookies. Cedar uses a session cookie to keep a signed-in reader authenticated while moving between library pages. The cookie expires when the browser session ends and is not used for advertising. A reader who blocks this cookie can browse public catalogue pages but cannot remain signed in to the private reading shelf. Essential cookies operate without optional-cookie consent because they support the requested sign-in function. The session cookie does not contain the text of a reader's saved notes. Signing out invalidates the server-side session even if a browser retains a stale local copy of the cookie until its next cleanup.

Security cookies. A separate security cookie helps distinguish ordinary requests from repeated automated abuse of the sign-in form. It expires after 24 hours and is classified as essential. The security cookie is not used to choose advertisements or to build a reading-interest profile. Clearing it may require the browser to complete an additional sign-in check, but does not delete the reader's account or saved shelf. Security controls should not be confused with optional traffic measurement: declining analytics does not disable the checks protecting sign-in. Cedar reviews security-cookie use when changing authentication flows so it remains limited to the protection of the service.

Analytics cookies. Optional analytics cookies measure which catalogue pages readers visit and how often navigation errors occur. They expire after 90 days unless the reader removes them earlier. Analytics cookies are set only after the reader gives analytics consent in the preference panel. Declining consent does not prevent access to public catalogue pages or an existing reading shelf. Analytics measurements are used to improve navigation, not to select advertisements for a particular reader. A cookie's expiry is the browser-side lifetime of that identifier; aggregate reporting has a separate retention rule. A member of staff testing navigation must respect the same consent setting as any other reader.

Aggregate measurement. Once optional analytics events have been stripped of browser identifiers and combined into aggregate counts, Cedar retains those aggregate reports for 13 months. The reports show totals such as visits to a catalogue category and do not allow staff to recover an individual reader's browsing path. The 13-month period applies to aggregate reports rather than the analytics cookie stored in a browser. Removing a cookie cannot remove a person's contribution from a report that no longer identifies them. Cedar uses these reports to compare seasonal navigation patterns and decide which parts of the catalogue need clearer signposting, without publishing individual reading histories.

Preference controls. Readers can change optional-cookie choices at any time through the Cookie Preferences link in the page footer. Withdrawal stops new optional analytics collection on that browser and causes Cedar to remove its optional analytics cookie there. The setting is browser-specific, so changing it on a laptop does not automatically change another browser on a phone. Existing aggregate reports are not deleted when consent is withdrawn because they no longer identify a reader. The preference cookie stores the chosen consent state for six months and is essential to remembering that choice. A reader can reopen the panel without creating an account or contacting support.

Embedded media. Some catalogue articles include a third-party video preview. The video provider's player loads only after a reader activates that preview and accepts the accompanying media consent prompt. If the reader declines, the article text and a plain link to the provider remain available. Cedar does not activate the player merely because the article was opened or because analytics consent was given. The video provider may then use its own cookies under its own policy, which the consent prompt links to. Accepting one video preview does not change the separate analytics preference stored for Cedar's catalogue pages.

Advertising and sharing. Cedar does not set advertising cookies and does not sell browsing-interest profiles to advertising networks. Optional analytics consent therefore does not opt a reader into personalised advertising. External sites linked from the catalogue may have their own advertising practices once a reader chooses to visit them. Cedar's policy describes tracking on its own catalogue and embedded-media controls, not a guarantee about every external destination. Library sponsors receive aggregate campaign counts, not a list of named readers or their saved shelves. Sponsorship of a reading list does not grant the sponsor access to account authentication cookies or private notes.

Browser maintenance. Most browsers allow a reader to remove stored cookies and control future cookie storage. Removing all cookies may sign the reader out and erase the remembered consent choice on that browser, so the preference panel may appear again. It does not delete the server-side account or saved reading shelf. The next visit can set essential cookies needed for a requested feature, while optional analytics still waits for consent. A private browsing window maintains a separate local cookie state from an ordinary window. Browser settings vary, so Cedar links to browser help rather than promise that every browser presents identical controls or menu names.
